Good to know before you go · 旅のヒント

5 things our guests learned in Japan

Every trip teaches something. These five lessons come from two of my guests, at the end of their 15-day trip across Japan — simple things that made their days easier. I now share them with everyone I plan for.

  1. Arrive during the day if you can

    Rather than at night, if at all possible. You are assured of much more help than at night — stations are staffed, people are around, and finding your hotel is simply easier.

  2. Always carry some cash

    In Japan, don't depend on your cards alone. Carry some cash, and carry your physical cards with you.

  3. Carry two or three ways to pay

    General travel advice, everywhere: at least two, probably three methods of payment. If one fails, your day doesn't stop.

  4. Don't be afraid to ask

    If you are stuck, ask. People will help you — this was true for my guests every time.

  5. Travel light, with a small suitcase

    You can wash your clothes as you go along. This was my guests' biggest lesson — they even forwarded their suitcases ahead (Japan's luggage forwarding services make this easy), and vowed to travel light from now on. I plan luggage forwarding into my itineraries for exactly this reason.

Afternoon Tea in Kyoto

From time to time, a trip deserves one special moment — and afternoon tea in Kyoto is one of my favourites. Most days of a trip stay simple and sensible; this is the pause we plan on purpose, inside the budget we agree together.

Three very different places are on my map:

  • Six Senses Kyoto — a calm, beautiful hotel tea
  • The Ritz-Carlton Kyoto — a grand hotel by the Kamo river

Places I love 大好きな場所

My Japan, personally visited.

Before it was a business, Bento Kumiko was where our family shared our favourite places in Japan. That hasn't changed — it's the heart of what I plan with. Every place here is somewhere I've been, loved, and would send my own family to.

Kyoto 京都

Old lanes, temples, tea houses — and the city I plan most carefully, because where you stay changes everything here.

Osaka 大阪

Come hungry. Osaka's back-street food shops and warm, funny welcome make it the city my guests talk about most.

Kumamoto 熊本

My Kyushu favourite — a great castle town, and a slower, friendlier side of Japan that many visitors miss.

Nagano 長野

Mountains, snow, and Zenkō-ji temple — Nagano is where I send travellers who want Japan beyond the big cities.

Every place personally visited — no paid placements, ever. I only recommend somewhere I know and love.
